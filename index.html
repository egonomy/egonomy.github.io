Egonomy: Scaling and Transferability Laws
of Human-Robot Co-training

A controlled empirical study of how 23,000 hours of human data scale and transfer into real-world robot policies.

01 · Abstract

Less Friction. More Transfer.

Scaling robot foundation models is bottlenecked by the cost of physical interaction data. Human activity data is the natural surrogate — but bridging the visual, kinematic, and dynamic embodiment gap is far from solved. We present Egonomy, a controlled empirical study of the scaling and transferability laws of human‑robot co‑training, spanning 23,000+ hours of human data and three action representations.

02 · TL;DR

Three Principal Findings

Scaling Laws & the Primacy of Diversity
Validation loss improves log-linearly with data volume; downstream success follows a quadratic-log trajectory. Environmental and task diversity drives generalization more than raw volume.
Transferability of Human-Robot Co-training
With a controlled co-training protocol, we observe robust transfer along spatial, semantic, object and scene axes — but only after large-scale pre-training.
Emergent Steerability & Limits
Co-training unlocks zero-shot inference-time steering: sparse human videos can dictate motion, target, and temporal structure. Truly novel or dexterous skills still require explicit kinematic grounding.

03 · Method

A Unified Co-training Recipe

We unify three forms of supervision under a single autoregressive policy: latent actions from in-the-wild video, MANO-recovered hand trajectories from egocentric video, and metrically grounded 7-DoF end-effector poses from UMI-like recordings. Each modality is decoded into plain text tokens, allowing all data sources to live in the same training mixture without bespoke heads. Co-training maximizes positive transfer across embodiments, while a small budget of in-domain robot data anchors the policy to executable controls.

04 · Data

The Human Data Spectrum

We instantiate a controlled trade-off across three forms of human supervision. Latent actions are maximally scalable but abstract; 3D hand trajectories are geometrically grounded yet bridge a hand-to-gripper gap; and UMI-like 7-DoF end-effector trajectories are metrically precise.

EgoVid. ~6,000 h of web-crawled POV video and ~2,000 h of self-collected head-mounted egocentric footage, automatically filtered, segmented and language-annotated.
Latent Action Model. VQ-VAE codes (size 32) capture motion-centric transitions between DINOv3-encoded frame pairs.
3D Hand Trajectory. A feed-forward pipeline fuses MANO wrist poses, π3 camera tracking and UniDepthV2 metric depth — no SLAM iteration required.

05 · Scaling

Predictable Scaling Laws

Across both offline metrics and real-world success rates, policy quality improves predictably as we add human data — no plateau is visible at our largest scale.

Success scaling. Across simulation and real benchmarks, downstream success follows a quadratic-log trajectory as pre-training data grows.
Loss scaling. Offline validation loss is log-linear in human data volume.
05
06 · Transfer

Four Axes of Transfer

Co-trained policies consistently outperform robot-only baselines on out-of-distribution evaluations. Different human data streams shape different generalization axes — and large-scale pre-training is indispensable.

Controlled four-axis evaluation. Robot data covers only in-domain conditions; human data injects out-of-distribution coverage along spatial, semantic, object and scene axes.
Post-training. Pre-training on diverse human data is what unlocks transfer; a small in-domain robot budget is enough to ground precise execution.
Co-training ablation. Latent actions drive semantic and visual robustness; trajectory supervision is required for spatial reasoning and articulated manipulation.
06
07 · Real-World

Real-World Manipulation

On a bimanual platform, Egonomy delivers consistent gains across both in-distribution and out-of-distribution settings — precisely where teleoperated robot data is least scalable.

In-distribution real-world tasks. A comprehensive task suite spans tool use, precise articulation, and non-prehensile manipulation. Egonomy matches or beats π0.5 across the vast majority of categories.
Out-of-distribution real-world tasks. Five generalization axes — long-horizon pick-and-place, skill, semantics, composition, and spatial — where the gap widens further.
07
08 · Emergent

Steerability & Skill Acquisition

Sufficient-scale co-training aligns representations enough that human videos can serve as behavioral context for the robot policy at inference time.

Inference-time visual steering. A handful of human video frames at inference can dictate motion direction, target object, and temporal ordering — with no weight updates.
